R-002 / ARCHIVED SUBMISSION

Ocean Acidification and Marine Ecosystems

An earlier independent research manuscript exploring threats posed by ocean acidification and possible responses.

Environmental researchSubmitted to NHSJSNot accepted · Archived

This was a real manuscript and journal submission, not merely an unfinished idea. It remains part of the archive because it shaped how I approached later research.

01 / Original question

What I set out to explore

The submitted manuscript was titled “To what extent does Ocean Acidification threaten the Marine Ecosystem, and what can we do about it?” It explored an environmental problem and possible ways to respond to it.

About this record: The original manuscript is not uploaded here. Its accompanying working-data dashboard reconstructs values and a simplified chemical model from contemporaneous notes; these are not independently validated ocean observations. The coral-cover series has an unresolved source gap.
02 / Editorial outcome

What the NHSJS decision said

NHSJS did not accept the manuscript. Its editorial message pointed to the level of conceptual advance, broader implications, and fit with the journal’s scope and readership when explaining its publication decision.

The letter did not give a detailed methodology-specific rejection or expressly challenge the validity of the study’s findings. It encouraged considering other journals that might be a better fit.

03 / Lessons

Why I am preserving this work

The experience made me think more carefully about what a research question adds to existing understanding, how to communicate significance, and how an idea fits an intended audience. I carried those lessons into my later Automation research, which underwent substantial methodological revision as well.

A journal rejection is a publication outcome, not the same thing as a determination that nothing was learned.

04 / Reconstructed research data

Explore the original data notes

The archived notes contain eight time points from 1960 through 2025 for atmospheric CO₂, an ocean-pH series, simplified calculated carbonate-ion concentrations, and proposed coral-cover values. The dashboard allows readers to inspect the year-by-year values and the original calculation assumptions.

Source caution: The coral-cover entries have not been traced to one valid, consistent observational source. The graphs show an early research reconstruction, not independently verified global coral loss or evidence of causation.
