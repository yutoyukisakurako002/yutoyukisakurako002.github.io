READING THE DATA

CO₂ rises; pH falls.

The researcher recorded atmospheric CO₂ increasing from 316.42 to 425.16 ppm, alongside an ocean pH series declining from 8.15 to 8.04. Some pH entries were explicitly described as calculated; this does not establish an independently observed global ocean trend.

Carbonate modelCoral-cover notes (unverified)

Left axis: μmol/kg; right axis: percent. Dashed amber line denotes the unverified coral-cover series.

READING THE DATA

A hypothesis — not a causal test.

The carbonate series was generated from a simplified fixed-condition chemical model. Coral-cover numbers were also recorded, but the research note does not establish a consistent observational source for 1960–2025. Do not treat this paired plot as evidence that acidification alone caused reef decline.

Reported carbonateRecomputed from rounded inputs

Two series share the same scale (μmol/kg).

READING THE DATA

The calculation is reproducible.

Recomputing the simplified model from documented constants and rounded CO₂/pH inputs gives values around 0.6–0.9 μmol/kg above the recorded series. That close agreement suggests a rounding/precision difference; it does not validate the model against real seawater measurements.

Method, original sources & limitations What is known — and what still needs verification

The chemical calculation in the original notes uses an approximate fixed-condition carbonate equilibrium model:

[CO₃²⁻] = K₀ · K₁ · K₂ · pCO₂ / [H⁺]²

Research-note constants: K₀ = 0.027 mol kg⁻¹ atm⁻¹, K₁ = 1.4 × 10⁻⁶, K₂ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁹. Assumed 25°C, salinity 35 PSU, pressure 1 atm. pCO₂ ≈ atmospheric ppm × 10⁻⁶ atm; [H⁺] = 10⁻pH. These are simplified research-note assumptions, not a complete ocean carbon chemistry model.

Source gap: The original notes contain eight annual markers for live coral cover, but the cited Great Barrier Reef paper analyzes 1985–2012. It cannot, by itself, justify a uniform worldwide coral-cover time series from 1960 to 2025. The reported change should not be interpreted as established fact until the underlying series and geographic scope are confirmed.

CO₂ and pH entries, chemical model constants, carbonate figures, and coral-cover entries are reconstructed from the original working document (not from a verified statistical database). No causal inference or independent validation is claimed.
